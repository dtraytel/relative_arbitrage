\title{Semicontinuity: Envelopes, Berge's Theorem and Measurable Selection}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Arguments in optimal control and in the theory of viscosity solutions work
  with functions that are only upper or lower semicontinuous, and repeatedly
  need their extrema on compacta, their semicontinuous envelopes, and a
  measurable choice of optimiser.  This entry assembles that toolkit for
  real-valued functions on a metric space, on top of the extended-real theory
  of the Archive entry \texttt{Lower\_Semicontinuous}.  We formalize the
  $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ calculus of semicontinuity, attainment of extrema on
  nonempty compacta, bounded extension off a closed set, the envelopes taken
  over the whole space and relative to a closed set, Berge's maximum theorem,
  that the value of a maximisation depends upper semicontinuously on its
  parameter, and the theorem of Bertsekas and Shreve~\cite{BertsekasShreve}
  that an upper semicontinuous payoff on a compact set admits a measurable
  maximiser.  The envelope is shown to be the hull of
  \texttt{Lower\_Semicontinuous} transported to the reals, so its basic
  theory is inherited rather than repeated, and the selection theorem is
  proved by greedy nested bisection, no countably valued
  $\varepsilon$-selector being available.
\end{abstract}
