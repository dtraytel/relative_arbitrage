\title{Semicontinuity: Envelopes, Berge's Theorem and Measurable Selection}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Arguments in optimal control and in the theory of viscosity solutions work
  with functions that are only upper or lower semicontinuous, and repeatedly
  need their extrema on compacta, their semicontinuous envelopes, and a
  measurable choice of optimiser.  HOL-Analysis provides none of this, and
  the Archive entry \texttt{Lower\_Semicontinuous} treats the extended-real
  hull of a convex function.  We formalize the $\varepsilon$-$\delta$
  calculus of semicontinuity for real-valued functions on a metric space,
  attainment of extrema on nonempty compacta, bounded extension off a closed
  set, the envelopes taken over the whole space and relative to a closed set,
  Berge's maximum theorem, and measurable selection of an upper
  semicontinuous payoff following Bertsekas and
  Shreve~\cite{BertsekasShreve}.  The envelope is shown to be the hull of
  \texttt{Lower\_Semicontinuous} transported to the reals, so its basic
  theory is inherited rather than repeated, and the selection theorem is
  proved by greedy nested bisection, no countably valued
  $\varepsilon$-selector being available.
\end{abstract}
