\title{Optional Sampling, Quadratic Variation and Transfer for Continuous-Time Martingales}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Arguments about continuous-time martingales sample a process at a stopping
  time, control it through its quadratic variation, and carry the martingale
  property along maps between measures.  Building on the Archive entry
  \texttt{Martingales}, which supplies the definitions and conditional
  expectation, this entry adds all three.  We formalize Doob's maximal
  inequality, bounding the chance that a martingale ever exceeds a level by
  its terminal mean over that level; optional sampling and optional stopping,
  that a martingale sampled or stopped at a bounded stopping time is again a
  martingale; quadratic variation together with its compensator; Vitali's
  convergence theorem, that a uniformly integrable sequence converging in
  probability converges in mean, and the uniform integrability of a family
  of conditional expectations; exit times of closed sets as stopping times,
  with bounds on their means from optional sampling; the semidirect product
  of a measure and a probability kernel, which continues a process by a law
  chosen at the point it has reached, with the almost-sure, rectangle and
  section calculus that disintegrates it; and a calculus for transporting
  the martingale property.  Sampling at a
  bounded stopping time, with events from the $\sigma$-algebra before it, is
  reduced to the discrete case by approximation through dyadic ceilings, and the transfer calculus covers image measures,
  products and infinite products, restriction to a full-measure event,
  conditioning on an event of the past, and modification.  Finally, we bound
  the second and fourth moments of the increments of a martingale whose
  compensated square grows at a bounded rate, iterating Cauchy--Schwarz and
  the tower property instead of invoking the Burkholder--Davis--Gundy
  inequality, and extend the fourth-moment bound from bounded to
  square-integrable martingales by localization: an $L^{2}$ martingale with
  continuous paths, stopped at any stopping time, is again a martingale, with
  no domination hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
