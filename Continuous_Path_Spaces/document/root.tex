\title{The Space of Continuous Paths, Tightness, and Pathwise Quadratic Variation}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Weak convergence of the laws of continuous stochastic processes takes place
  on a space of continuous paths, and identifying a limit requires a
  criterion for tightness.  The Archive entry
  \texttt{Levy\_Prokhorov\_Metric} supplies weak convergence and Prokhorov's
  theorem over an abstract Polish space, but neither that path space nor a
  criterion applicable to path laws.  We build $C([0,T],\,'b)$ under the
  supremum metric as a Polish space in which H\"older balls are compact,
  derive tightness of a family of path laws from second and fourth moment
  bounds on the increments by dyadic chaining and H\"older interpolation, and
  construct quadratic variation as a pathwise Borel functional.  The
  fourth-moment estimate is obtained by iterating Cauchy--Schwarz and the
  tower property rather than from the Burkholder--Davis--Gundy inequality,
  and the quadratic variation is regularised to a version that is adapted and
  continuous along every path, its $L^{2}$ rate being summable enough to
  converge without extracting a subsequence.
\end{abstract}
