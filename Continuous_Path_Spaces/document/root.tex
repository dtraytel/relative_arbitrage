\title{The Space of Continuous Paths, Tightness, and Pathwise Quadratic Variation}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Weak convergence of laws of continuous processes needs a Polish state
  space and a tightness criterion.  This entry supplies both, and a pathwise
  construction of quadratic variation.

  The space $C([0,T],\,'b)$ of continuous paths with the supremum metric is
  built as a metric space in the set-based form of the Archive's
  \texttt{Standard\_Borel\_Spaces} entry, and shown separable and complete.
  H\"older balls in it are compact --- Arzel\`a--Ascoli in the form needed
  later.  The path map of a stochastic process is measurable into it, so a
  process has a law on path space, and these laws are consistent across
  horizons.  The continuous mapping theorem and two portmanteau facts are
  proved for weak convergence of such laws, on top of the weak convergence of
  the \texttt{Levy\_Prokhorov\_Metric} entry.

  The tightness criterion is a moment estimate.  For a martingale whose
  compensator grows at a controlled rate, second and fourth moments of the
  increments are bounded on a partition; a dyadic chaining argument turns the
  fourth-moment bound into a tail bound for the modulus of continuity, and
  H\"older interpolation converts that into a bound on a H\"older seminorm.
  Since H\"older balls are compact, the family of laws is tight, hence
  relatively compact by Prokhorov's theorem from that same entry.  The
  fourth-moment estimate avoids the Burkholder--Davis--Gundy inequality,
  iterating Cauchy--Schwarz and the tower property instead; the resulting
  constant is smaller than the usual route gives~\cite{LaiShkolnikovSoner}.

  Quadratic variation is then built as a \emph{path functional} rather than
  by Doob--Meyer: the pathwise limit of dyadic sums, with an $L^{2}$ rate
  that is summable, so the full dyadic sequence converges almost surely and
  no subsequence is needed.  It is regularised to a version that is adapted
  and continuous for \emph{every} path, which is what makes the lift
  $w \mapsto (w, \langle w\rangle)$ land in a space of continuous paths
  without breaking the filtration.  Localisation at a stopping time, needed
  because members of a class of laws need not be bounded, is proved
  unconditionally for $L^{2}$ martingales with continuous paths.
  Billingsley~\cite{Billingsley} is the textbook reference for the tightness
  argument.
\end{abstract}
