\title{The Space of Continuous Paths, Tightness, and Pathwise Quadratic Variation}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Weak convergence of the laws of continuous stochastic processes takes place
  on a space of continuous paths, and identifying a limit requires a
  criterion for tightness.  This entry supplies both, on top of the weak
  convergence and Prokhorov theorem of the Archive entry
  \texttt{Levy\_Prokhorov\_Metric}.  We build $C([0,T],\,'b)$ under the
  supremum metric as a Polish space in which H\"older balls are compact, by
  the Arzel\`a--Ascoli theorem, and
  prove that a family of path laws whose increments obey uniform second and
  fourth moment bounds is tight, hence relatively compact, by dyadic chaining
  and H\"older interpolation; quadratic variation is then constructed as a
  pathwise Borel functional, the limit of dyadic sums along every path.  The
  moment estimates come from the entry
  \texttt{Continuous\_Time\_Martingales}, which obtains the fourth moment
  without the Burkholder--Davis--Gundy inequality, so that any sequence of
  square-integrable martingales with continuous paths, started at a common
  point, whose compensators grow at a common bounded rate has a subsequence
  whose path laws converge weakly.
  A path whose difference quotients over rational times lie in a closed set
  has all its difference quotients there.
  The quadratic variation is regularised to a version that is adapted and
  continuous along every path, its $L^{2}$ rate being summable enough to
  converge without extracting a subsequence; by localisation it equals the
  compensator of a continuous $L^{2}$ martingale whose compensator grows at
  a bounded rate, with no boundedness hypothesis on the martingale.
  Finally, the exit time of a closed set is upper semicontinuous on the path
  space, so its essential infimum is upper semicontinuous along weak
  convergence of path laws, as in Lemma~2.1 of Larsson and
  Ruf~\cite{LarssonRuf}; shifting the set by a starting point, the supremum
  of that essential infimum over a weakly compact family of laws is upper
  semicontinuous in the starting point, by Berge's maximum theorem.
  For optimisation over path
  laws, the entry also proves the theorem of Bertsekas and
  Shreve~\cite{BertsekasShreve} that an upper semicontinuous payoff on a
  compact set admits a measurable maximiser, by greedy nested
  bisection along a countable dense sequence, no countably valued
  $\varepsilon$-selector being available.
\end{abstract}
