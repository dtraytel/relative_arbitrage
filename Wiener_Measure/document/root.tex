\title{Wiener Measure as a Projective Limit}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Brownian motion is constructed in Isabelle/HOL as a measure on the space of
  real-valued functions of time, together with a continuous modification.

  The construction is the classical one.  The Gaussian increment measure of a
  given variance is defined, with the degenerate variance giving a point
  mass, and its moments and convolution law are established, the latter both
  as a pushforward and in iterated-integral form.  Finite-dimensional
  distributions are then pushforwards of finite products of independent
  increment measures under cumulative-sum maps, and projectivity of that
  family reduces to merging two adjacent increments, that is, to the
  convolution law.  Wiener measure is the projective limit, obtained from the
  Daniell--Kolmogorov theorem already available in \texttt{HOL-Probability}.
  Its increments are shown independent and Gaussian, and the fourth moment
  $\mathbb{E}\,|B_t - B_s|^{4} = 3\,(t-s)^{2}$ is computed.

  That moment bound discharges the hypothesis of the Kolmogorov--Chentsov
  theorem from the Archive entry of the same name, which yields a modification
  of the coordinate process with continuous paths: Brownian motion in the
  usual sense.  See Karatzas and Shreve~\cite{KaratzasShreve} for the
  textbook account.
\end{abstract}
