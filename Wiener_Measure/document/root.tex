\title{Wiener Measure as a Projective Limit}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Brownian motion is the basic object of continuous-time stochastic analysis.
  Isabelle had no construction of it: the Archive entry
  \texttt{Kolmogorov\_Chentsov} supplies a criterion for the existence of a
  continuous modification, but no measure to apply it to.  We construct
  Wiener measure as the projective limit of finite-dimensional distributions
  assembled from Gaussian increments, prove the increments independent and
  Gaussian, compute the fourth moment
  $\mathbb{E}\,|B_t - B_s|^{4} = 3\,(t-s)^{2}$, and obtain from it a
  modification with continuous paths.  Projectivity is reduced to the
  convolution law for Gaussian densities by merging two adjacent increments,
  which brings the construction within reach of the Daniell--Kolmogorov
  theorem already available in \texttt{HOL-Probability}.  We follow the
  account of Karatzas and Shreve~\cite{KaratzasShreve}.
\end{abstract}
