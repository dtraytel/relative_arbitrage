\title{Sup-Convolutions, Alexandrov's Theorem, and Comparison of Viscosity Solutions}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Comparison between a viscosity subsolution and a supersolution of a fully
  nonlinear degenerate elliptic equation rests on second-order
  differentiability of functions that are merely semiconvex.  Neither
  Rademacher's theorem nor Alexandrov's theorem had been formalized in
  Isabelle, placing any such comparison principle out of reach.  We formalize
  both, together with Jensen's lemma for semiconvex functions and the
  Crandall--Ishii theorem on sums, over a base of subgradients, the proximal
  map, the Moreau envelope and the sup-convolution, and add the
  doubling-of-variables apparatus these results exist to serve, along with
  coercive penalties whose second-order jet vanishes on the diagonal.
  Alexandrov's theorem is obtained by Minty's device, applying Rademacher to
  the resolvent of the subdifferential and transporting the expansion back
  from the Moreau envelope; the doubling toolbox is stated over an arbitrary
  Euclidean space, matrices appearing only where a statement produces one.
  We follow Evans and Gariepy~\cite{EvansGariepy} for the differentiability
  theorems and Crandall, Ishii and Lions~\cite{CrandallIshiiLions} for the
  rest.
\end{abstract}
