\title{The Spectral Theory of Real Symmetric Matrices}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Many optimisation and control problems constrain a real symmetric matrix
  through its spectrum --- through a bound on a sum of its largest
  eigenvalues, say --- rather than through its entries.  HOL-Analysis carries
  symmetric matrices as far as their quadratic forms; this entry takes them
  to their eigenvalues.  We formalize the spectral theorem, that every real
  symmetric matrix has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors, by maximising
  the Rayleigh quotient over invariant subspaces, and Ky Fan's theorem, that
  the sum of the $m$ largest eigenvalues is the largest trace of the matrix
  against a rank-$m$ orthogonal projection; the ordered eigenvalues follow as
  differences of consecutive Ky Fan sums, are Lipschitz in the matrix, and
  interlace with those of any compression to a subspace, which is Poincar\'e
  separation.  Supporting all of this is a calculus of trace, transpose,
  outer products, orthonormal families and Householder rotations.  Defining
  the Ky Fan sums by maximisation over projections keeps the ordered
  eigenvalues basis-free and avoids any well-definedness argument about
  sorting a multiset.
\end{abstract}
