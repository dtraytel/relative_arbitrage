\title{The Spectral Theory of Real Symmetric Matrices}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  The spectral theory of real symmetric matrices that \texttt{HOL-Analysis}
  stops short of, for anyone who constrains a matrix through its spectrum
  rather than through its entries.  The Archive has no diagonalisation of a
  real symmetric matrix; \texttt{Jordan\_Normal\_Form} and
  \texttt{Hilbert\_Space\_Tensor\_Product} address different settings.

  A calculus of trace, transpose and the two matrix products comes first,
  together with outer products, orthonormal families and their completeness
  relation, and the Householder reflections and the two-reflection rotation
  used to transport a frame.  The spectral theorem itself is proved by the
  variational argument: a maximiser of the Rayleigh quotient on an invariant
  subspace is an eigenvector, and induction on dimension peels off an
  orthonormal eigenbasis.

  On top of it, Ky Fan's theorem is developed in the form needed to constrain
  a matrix by its ordered eigenvalues without ever naming them individually:
  the sum of the $m$ largest eigenvalues is characterised as a supremum of
  traces against orthogonal projections, the individual ordered eigenvalues
  are recovered as differences of consecutive such sums, and both are shown
  Lipschitz in the matrix.  The Poincar\'e separation inequality compares the
  spectrum of a matrix with that of its compression to a subspace, via
  Courant--Fischer.

  Nothing in the entry refers to any particular equation.  It was extracted
  from a formalisation of the uniqueness half of a relative-arbitrage
  problem~\cite{LaiShkolnikovSoner}, where a lower bound on a sum of ordered
  eigenvalues is one of the two spectral constraints defining the problem's
  elliptic operator.
\end{abstract}
