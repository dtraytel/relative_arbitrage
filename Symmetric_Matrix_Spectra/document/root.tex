\title{The Spectral Theory of Real Symmetric Matrices}
\author{Dmitriy Traytel}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  Many optimisation and control problems constrain a real symmetric matrix
  through its spectrum --- through a bound on a sum of its largest
  eigenvalues, say --- rather than through its entries.  HOL-Analysis
  diagonalises no symmetric matrix, and the existing matrix entries of the
  Archive treat other settings, so such a constraint cannot even be stated.
  We formalize the spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices by the
  variational argument, together with the supporting calculus of trace,
  transpose, outer products, orthonormal families and Householder rotations,
  and develop Ky Fan's theorem: the sum of the $m$ largest eigenvalues as a
  supremum of traces against orthogonal projections, the ordered eigenvalues
  as differences of consecutive such sums, their Lipschitz dependence on the
  matrix, and the Poincar\'e separation inequality via Courant--Fischer.
  Characterising the eigenvalues in this way keeps them basis-free and avoids
  any well-definedness argument about sorting a multiset.
\end{abstract}
